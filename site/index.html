      What recent reports point to

      Summary as of 25 October 2026, drawn from the reports listed below. Each point says which report it comes from.

      • Growth is picking up. The World Bank estimates growth of 5.1% in 2025, up from 4.0% in 2024 (Macro Poverty Outlook, April 2026). The IMF, which credits mining, construction and manufacturing, projects 5.5% for 2026 (September 2026). The African Development Bank puts 2025 lower, at 4.6% (Country Focus Report, July 2026), so estimates differ. The World Bank's October 2026 update also projects 5.5% for 2026.
      • Aid is falling and squeezing the budget. Grants fell from 7.8% of GDP in 2024 to 5.1% in 2025, and the World Bank says declining external aid is tightening fiscal space (April 2026). In this viewer's OECD data, gross aid to Liberia fell from US$1.14bn in 2015 to US$524m in 2024.
      • US aid cuts hit hardest. US aid was close to 2.6% of Liberia's national income, the highest share of any country, according to the Center for Global Development. About US$290m of the US$443m expected for 2025 was affected, with health (malaria, maternal health, HIV) worst hit (Africanews, July 2025).
      • More budget money now comes from the IMF and World Bank. The IMF's Extended Credit Facility (since September 2024) and its SDR 193.8m (about US$265m) Resilience and Sustainability Facility for climate, approved in April 2026, passed their latest reviews in September 2026. The World Bank approved US$55.8m of budget support in June 2026 to help close an "emerging financing gap".
      • Public finances have tightened. The deficit narrowed to about 1–2% of GDP in 2025 (World Bank 1.1%, AfDB 1.8%) and public debt is about 52–55% of GDP. The IMF says a VAT is due in 2027 alongside mining tax reform (September 2026).
      • Poverty remains high. 35.8% of Liberians lived on less than US$3 a day in 2025, projected to fall to 29.8% by 2028 (World Bank, April 2026).
      • Risks named across the reports: declining donor support and concessional finance, commodity and fuel prices, governance and capacity, and climate shocks.
      • The plan: the government's ARREST Agenda for Inclusive Development 2025–2029 targets average growth of 5.9% a year (January 2025). The African Development Bank has a new country strategy for 2026–2031.

      Caveat. This is a short summary of other organisations' published reports, made for this tool and checked against them on 25 October 2026. It is not an assessment by this tool or by any government, and it may not reflect reports or revisions published since. The figures come from different sources and dates and don't always agree, and some (the US aid figures) come from press reporting. Read the reports themselves before relying on any figure.
